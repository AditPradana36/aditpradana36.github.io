Green open spaces (GOS) in cities play a significant role in providing space to catch one's breath in hyper-dense metropolitan areas. GOS, or what we could simply call greenery, should be a staple feature of cities in order to fulfil people's needs. But eventually, what if your greenery could be replaced by a cemetery? Well, it would still be green on the surface, though. The real question is: would you like to spend your leisure time in a cemetery? Probably not. _If you would, you might be interested in urban legends or something similar._

Well, back to the real issue, where legal instruments are responsible for defining greenery and translating it into actionable practices, while allowing this loose definition to persist. In this blog, I would like to bring you into a discussion about how greenery is not really an important provision, or is perhaps nearly unimportant, in Indonesia's legal framework, especially where it overlaps with the spatial domain.

# "Space" as more than just a measurable land

The fact that Indonesia's spatial legal framework still put an eyes at greenery provision as an environment savior and a public need by **mandating 30% of city's land must be provided for greenery**[^first]. Imagine that one-third of city area might have been a greenery area. But, apparently, this mandate is not something that every city must fulfill on the next day. The mandate is more likely a set of goal without any deadline attached to it.

[^first]: Government of the Republic of Indonesia (2007) Law of the Republic of Indonesia No. 26 of 2007 on Spatial Planning. Available at: [LINK](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/39908/uu-no-26-tahun-2007)

The mandate leaves one important caveat. **It did not differ what people's need and spatial governance trying to achieve.** Consequently, every city could have their own measurement by enabling cemetery, planted road median, etc. Which can be counted as a greenery---by the surface of them, such a creative interpretation there, to be fair.

# Jakarta as a good (and bad at the same time) example

Jakarta actually had done a pretty good job in providing GOS across its jurisdiction. Spatially, the existence of GOS is evenly spread if you see it from the above. It has been published by Jakarta Government it self[^second] in form of WebGIS---_you'd better check it out first!_. The numbers are not superior but the effort should be praised.

[^second]: DKI Jakarta Provincial Government (2026) Green Open Space Information in DKI Jakarta Province. Available at: [LINK](https://jakartasatu.jakarta.go.id/portal/apps/dashboards/d4432a1cc031400c8e8f610345022f81).

Just wander how come Jakarta was able to provide all those greeneries alongside hyper-dense metropolitan environment. And yes, that's it how I praise Jakarta in this regard. Nothing else. One thing that we can put blame is how Jakarta deliberately includes cemetery, road median, etc which have nothing to do with park-like greenery. Like I said earlier where **cemetery can't be included as public space in literal meaning.** As well as the roads median, they are green on the surface, but technically---and obviously---unreachable. Who is going to put a bench in the middle of 2 lanes road? No one.

What I'm trying to say here **the green** it self not just merely a surface; it needs to be defined with the other 2 words simultaneously---_"open"_ and _"space"_.
