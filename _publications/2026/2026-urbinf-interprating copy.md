abstract: >-
  Indonesia's Decree No. 39/M/KEP/2026 bars single-authored journal articles from serving as qualifying publications for academic promotion. Objections to such rules are readily dismissed on the grounds that scholarship has moved decisively toward collaboration, but that trend does not hold across all fields, and the exception is where the rule does its damage. The restriction is also narrower than commonly reported: such articles still earn credit, but no quantity of them substitutes for one co-authored qualifying work, making these publications the real bottleneck in every promotion pathway. The decree also specifies author position, requiring candidates to be first or first-and-corresponding author. This combination forecloses honorary and guest authorship while creating demand for co-authors who do not compete for the lead position, namely junior colleagues, supervised staff, and peers in reciprocal arrangements. Indonesian academics have already shown they respond to evaluation metrics by expanding author lists. Measured against COPE and ICMJE criteria, the difficulty is that the decree verifies only what the byline displays and requires no account of contribution, so a name earned through substantial work and a name added for compliance are indistinguishable at assessment. A regulation intended to strengthen research thus weakens the record of who is accountable for it. This article argues for discipline-sensitive qualifying criteria alongside stronger verification, including contribution statements submitted with promotion dossiers and institutional monitoring of authorship patterns.
